Inflation is mostly about money

Stimulus poured into an economy with slack fills the output gap and lifts real output. Poured into an economy that is already near potential, it overflows into prices. The post-COVID wave is the case study: pour the stimulus into the hole and watch how much overflows. Friedman’s line still bites — and the Fed financed most of the money.

The spine, and the debate. Money is a good; flood the economy with it and its purchasing power (1/P) falls — that fall is inflation, and most of the $6.3T money growth was the Fed financing the stimulus. The demand-pull view (Summers, Blanchard): ~$5T poured into a ~$0.7T hole had to overflow into prices once the economy hit potential. The supply view (Bivens/EPI): the gap stayed negative into late 2021, yet COVID skewed demand to goods and broke supply chains, with energy and the 2022 Ukraine shock on top. The decomposition lets you weigh both. Calibrated to the U.S. episode (M2 +41%; ARP $1.9T; early-2021 output gap ≈ $0.7T per CBO/Brookings; SF Fed est. fiscal added ~3pp to core; CPI peak 9.1% Jun 2022; ~18-month money-to-inflation lag). Stylised for teaching, not an econometric forecast.

1 · MV = PY is an identity, not a theory

M × V  =  P × Y

Velocity is never measured. It is defined as a residual: V = PY / M — nominal GDP divided by the money stock. That is exactly how the published M2 velocity series is constructed. So the equation is true by construction and cannot be falsified by any data.

The economic claim — the part that can be wrong — is the added assumption that V is stable and Y is set by real factors. Only then does more M mean higher P. When velocity moves, the identity still holds perfectly while the theory quietly stops predicting anything. That is the whole story of 2008 versus 2020.

Teaching point: an unfalsifiable accounting identity is doing the rhetorical work of an economic law. Ask which part of “inflation is always and everywhere a monetary phenomenon” is definition and which is claim.

Read it as the identity demands: in 2009 M rose and V fell almost one-for-one, so PY barely moved — no inflation. In 2021–23 M rose and then V rebounded while Y was capped by capacity, so P had to absorb it.

3 · Where the money came from — and in what form

The decisive difference is not how much money was created but whose account it landed in. Both episodes were called “money printing”; only one put spendable purchasing power in households’ hands.

2008 — QE creates reserves

  1. Fed buys Treasuries & MBS from banks
  2. Banks receive reserves held at the Fed
  3. Reserves are not part of M2 — they cannot be spent in the shops
  4. Deleveraging, weak loan demand and interest paid on reserves kept them parked

An asset swap: the composition of bank balance sheets changed, household purchasing power did not. M2 grew modestly, V fell, P barely moved.

2020 — deficits create deposits

  1. Treasury borrows and sends transfers, top-ups and PPP
  2. Money appears directly as deposits in household and firm accounts
  3. Deposits are M2 — immediately spendable
  4. The Fed bought roughly a matching quantity of Treasuries, so the deficit was in effect money-financed

New purchasing power in the hands of people who spent it — Friedman’s helicopter money, literally. M2 +41%, and once V rebounded, P followed.

The form of M matters as much as the quantity. “The Fed printed money” describes both years and explains neither. Reserves circulate between banks; deposits circulate through the economy.

4 · How the peak is calculated

The simulator is deliberately simple and additive — three components, no dynamics:

peak inflation  =  baseline (2.0%)  +  0.81 × max(0, stimulus − gap)  +  supply shock

Only stimulus in excess of the gap creates demand-pull — money poured into slack becomes real output, not prices. That is the beaker: below the rim it fills the hole, above the rim it overflows.

Honest caveat on the coefficient. The 0.81pp per $T is calibrated, not estimated — chosen so the actual 2021–23 settings reproduce the observed peak, anchored to the SF Fed’s finding that fiscal support added roughly 3pp to core inflation. It is a teaching parameter that encodes a contested empirical claim, not an independent measurement. A student who disputes the demand-pull share should dispute this number.

Notice the mechanism in row three: with Y at its ceiling and V recovering, the identity leaves P as the only variable free to move. That is the whole quantity-theory intuition in one line — and why the same M growth in 2009 did nothing.

6 · What this model does not do

  • No dynamics or lags. The real money-to-inflation lag ran roughly 18 months; here everything resolves instantly.
  • No expectations. Anchored expectations are a large part of why this ended without a 1970s spiral. Absent here.
  • Additive, not interactive. Demand and supply shocks are summed; in reality they interact (excess demand makes broken supply chains bite harder).
  • The gap is an input, not an outcome. You set the hole; a real model would let stimulus, potential and the gap co-evolve.
  • Velocity is not modelled at all in the simulator — it is the residual discussed above, which is precisely why the money story needs the caveats on this page.
Use the limits as the discussion: each one is a place where a confident claim about 2021 inflation depends on an assumption rather than a measurement.
