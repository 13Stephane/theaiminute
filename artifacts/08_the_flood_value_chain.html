Managerial Economics · Day 2 · Team exercise

The flood

Take the company from your strategy project. Break its value chain into three or four stages, judge how hard each is for AI to do, then raise the water. Whatever the water covers stops being scarce — and stops being paid for. What is left standing is where the value goes.

1 · Draw the chain

Three or four stages, from raw input to the customer. The chain that delivers the value — not the org chart. Mark the stage your company actually stands on.

2 · Height each dam, then raise the water

Height 0–100 = how hard the stage is for AI. Low: pattern recognition, drafting, analysis, coordination. High: physical assets, regulated licence, accountability, trust, proprietary data, real-world experiment.
How much of the work can AI already do to a standard your customers accept?
Be honest, and say why. This number is the argument.

3 · Read the economics

Every stage is a small market. Same machinery, applied stage by stage.

4 · Can your stage escape the margin?

Score the three conditions at the stage you stand on. Price detaches from marginal cost only where one of them is broken.

5 · The claim you would defend

One sentence, and the thing that would prove you wrong.

How to argue with it. The heights are judgements, not measurements — that is the point of doing this in a team. Two disciplines: name what a dam is actually made of (an asset, a licence, an accountability, a dataset), and say what would lower it. A dam you cannot describe is usually not there. Stylised for teaching. Every number here is a claim someone should be able to defend.
