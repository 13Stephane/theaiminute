Managerial Economics · Day 2 · The flood

The flood, the wall, and the way out

On Day 1 you took a job apart and asked which of its tasks the machine would take. This is the next question: once those tasks are cheap, who gets paid? Three pieces of work, then one round of feedback on all of it. First the flood: the main activities in your industry, and your team's vote on which the machine will take. Then the wall: what you own that the water cannot reach. Then the way out: how you escape the margin three years from now. When all three are done, send the lot for feedback, to Claude from this page or to any assistant you prefer.

About 30 minutes. The flood 7, the wall 7, the way out 5, feedback 6, then a five-minute round. Do the three pieces of work before asking for feedback: the feedback is on everything at once.
1
The flood
about 7 minutes
Not the tasks in one job this time, but the main activities of your industry: the stages a customer’s money passes through. Seven at most. Each member of the team votes once per activity, with the same three choices as Day 1: automate, augment, or keep human. Reach is automate plus half of augment. An activity a strict majority votes to keep human stays out of the flood.

The wall
Teeceabout 7 minutes
What keeps the water out. Name the three or four pieces of intellectual property and assets that matter most, the ones that stay valuable when the activities above flood. For each, say what kind it is, whether it is bound to you (a rival cannot rent or copy it within three years) or generic, and which escape from the margin it makes possible. Teece's rule: where the idea can be copied, value goes to whoever owns the assets it has to pass through.
3
The way out, three years from now
about 5 minutes
Price only stays above cost if something stops buyers treating you as interchangeable, stops them moving between prices, or stops rivals entering. Choose the escape you will actually pursue, name the move, and say which card on the wall it depends on.
4

about 6 minutes
One prompt carries your flood, your wall and your way out, together with the framework you used. The feedback will challenge your calls, test whether your wall is really yours, and ask whether your escape follows from it. Read it as a team, then decide what you disagree with.

What your data says
Up to six readings, written from your votes, your wall, your way out and the feedback. Prompts for the round, not verdicts.

What this will not do. It does not forecast. Your votes are public inside the team, not the private sort a room would give. Feedback from any assistant is one model's view: it knows nothing your team knows about your firm, and two assistants can disagree. The argument between their read and yours is the point.
