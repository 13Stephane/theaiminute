Reading the charts. Six charts per team: the US row in red, the euro area in blue. The dashed grey line behind each is what actually happened in 2020–21. On the unemployment charts, the gold dotted line is that economy’s natural rate (US 4.0%, euro area 7.2%) — scoring counts only the distance above that line, because 6.6% is normal in Europe and would be a crisis in America. This is why a team can end with higher euro-area unemployment and still score better on it. Every series starts at its pre-COVID baseline. Teams are scored on stabilisation, not on tracking the grey line — reality was not optimal.

How the score works. Team stabilisation loss = average inflation gap from 2% + average unemployment above natural + output-gap distance at the end, for both blocs, plus penalties for average financial stress and any euro-area spread above 1pp; mapped to 0–100. Unemployment counts every quarter — letting joblessness spike mid-crisis costs points even if it recovers by end-2021. Each role is scored against its own mandate (Fed: US inflation + unemployment; ECB: euro-area inflation + the spread; US Gov: recovery without overheating or excess debt; EU Govs: recovery + spread discipline). Scored against stabilisation, never against matching what actually happened — reality wasn’t optimal. A rigorous individual-contribution measure (counterfactual re-runs) can be added if you want true attribution.
