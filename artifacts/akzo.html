Where each part earns its spread

Specialty Chemicals

  • Mature chemistries sold to specification: ethylene and sulfur derivatives, industrial chemicals, pulp and performance chemicals, polymer and surface chemistry.
  • Industry evolution: stage 3. Competition runs on process and cost; value sits with the cost leader.
  • Teece: the complementary assets decide, over 70 manufacturing sites and 15 research centres. IP matters in pockets (polymer, surface chemistry), not across the portfolio.
  • Theory of value: operational efficiency. Natural owner: a buyer that prizes cash conversion and can carry debt.

Paints & Coatings

  • Brands (Dulux) and specifications: #1 in marine, #2 in aerospace and industrial coatings (case, appendix 1).
  • Industry evolution: mature and consolidating. PPG 12%, AkzoNobel 9% of a fragmented market; Sherwin-Williams buying Valspar for $11.3 bn. This is horizontal scale, Utterback's shakeout, not stage-4 re-integration.
  • Teece: the spread goes to whoever holds brands, channels and scale. AkzoNobel's decorative margins sat below peers (appendix 1).
  • Theory of value: intangible capital, with scale as the missing piece. PPG's premium was a price for that scale.

The executive-board exercise

From the case exercise: it is 18 October 2017, the stock trades at €58 (about 2.1× book), PPG offers €89 in cash, and the CEO's proposed structure sells Specialty Chemicals for €10,100 m, 100% paid out. You ask the supervisory board for a yes or a no.

1. At €58, what is AkzoNobel's EV/EBITDA?
Market value 252 m × €58 = €14,616 m. Add interest-bearing debt of €2,731 m (end 2016): EV €17,347 m. Divide by 2016 EBITDA of €2,242 m (EBIT €1,519 m + D&A €723 m): 7.7×. Check: €14,616 m ÷ equity €7,034 m = 2.08× book.
2. At the 11.5× peer multiple, what would the whole company be worth per share?
11.5 × €2,242 m = €25,783 m EV. Less €2,731 m debt = €23,052 m equity, ÷ 252 m = €91.5 per share, +58% on €58. The discount to peers alone is worth more than PPG's €89.
3. After the sale, what is the upside, and is it enough?
Paints & Coatings: 11.5 × €1,349 m = €15,514 m EV, less €3,585 m debt = €11,929 m, ÷ 252 m = €47.3. Specialty Chemicals: €10,100 m ÷ 252 m = €40.1. Sum: €87.4, +50.7% on €58, against PPG's €89, +53.4%. Separation needs Paints & Coatings to re-rate to about 11.8× just to match the offer, before tax, pensions and separation costs.
4. Sell Specialty Chemicals, or accept PPG?
On these numbers the two paths are within €2 a share. The choice therefore turns on what the numbers leave out: certainty (cash now against a re-rating that may not come), leakage on the sale, PPG's synergies (which a premium would share with you), and what a sale would cost the other stakeholders: 46,000 jobs and the Dutch ties the Boards cited. The board's vote is a judgment about those, not a calculation.

What the sum of the parts leaves out

  • PPG's synergies, and how much of them the premium already hands to AkzoNobel's shareholders.
  • Separation costs and stranded corporate costs: 46 legal entities were shared between the two businesses (circular, basis of preparation).
  • Tax on the sale and pension funding, which the circular excludes from net proceeds.
  • Timing and risk: proceeds arrive in 2018; PPG's stock component moves with PPG; PPG's offer carried antitrust risk.
  • The re-rating of Paints & Coatings is an assumption about the market, not a cash flow.
