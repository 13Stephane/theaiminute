Work in the Age of AI · Brynjolfsson

No job is automated — only its tasks

A role is a bundle of tasks. AI rarely takes a whole job; it reshapes the bundle. Decompose a role, sort each task — automate, augment, or keep human — then weight each by its share of the week and of the role’s value. Counting tasks flatters automation; weighting reveals where the time and the worth actually sit. Tap a card to re-classify; drag to re-weight.

Automation caps value. Augmentation expands it.

Replacing what humans already do puts an upper bound on the gain. Letting AI extend what humans couldn’t do before opens new value — and tends to lift wages rather than concentrate them.

Mind the Turing Trap.

The easy instinct is to point AI at tasks humans already do, bank the saving, and call it progress. The richer move is to ask what becomes possible that no one was doing — and who owns the scarce complement once the task itself is cheap.

How to run it in class. Each group decomposes a real role from their own organisation, corrects Claude’s weights to match their week, then debates the split. Watch the headline move as you switch from task count to time to value: the tasks that resist automation are usually the few that carry the role’s worth. The radiologist example is Brynjolfsson’s — AI strong at reading images, weak at the human encounter. Weights are estimates to argue with, not measurements.
