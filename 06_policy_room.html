Before you start — what each seat is graded on

You are scored on stabilisation: keeping both economies near 2% inflation and full employment across the whole game — not on copying what actually happened. Reality was not optimal, and beating it is expected. Each seat also has its own mandate:
Federal Reserve — US rate + QE. Judged on US inflation near 2% and unemployment near 4%.
ECB — euro rate + QE. Judged on euro-area inflation and keeping the periphery spread near 1pp.
US Government — stimulus size + composition. Judged on recovery without overheating; a large debt surge costs points.
Euro-area Governments — stimulus size + composition. Judged on recovery plus spread discipline.
Two things to internalise before Q1: inflation answers your decisions 3–6 quarters later, so you are always steering ahead of the gauges. And your mandates conflict by design — the Fed wants price stability while the Treasury wants a fast recovery. Negotiate; do not default to the average call. Hide this

The model behind the room

Every equation the simulation uses, in one place. All flows are quarterly; two economies (US, EU) are solved each quarter, then coupled through trade, markets, and sentiment. This is a teaching model \u2014 directionally faithful, not a forecasting tool.

1 \u00B7 Output \u2014 an IS\u2013LM reduced form

Y = 0.40\u00B7Y\u208B\u2081 + pandemic + 0.60\u00B7fiscal \u2212 0.30\u00B7(realRate \u2212 r*) + 0.10\u00B7QE + 0.15\u00B7Y_other + 0.06\u00B7sentiment

IS curve: output falls when the real rate rises above its neutral level r* (\u22120.5% US, \u22121.0% EU). LM / balance-sheet channel: QE adds demand directly \u2014 the lever that still works when the policy rate is stuck at zero. realRate = policy rate \u2212 inflation. Output is then squashed through a tanh so extreme stimulus saturates rather than exploding.

Y = 14\u00B7tanh(Y / 14)

2 \u00B7 Fiscal impulse \u2014 size \u00D7 composition, with inertia

fiscalRaw = stim \u00D7 \u03A3(mix\u2096 \u00B7 demand\u2096) fiscal = 0.25\u00B7fiscalRaw + 0.60\u00B7fiscalRaw\u208B\u2081

Stimulus size (% GDP) times the demand-potency of its composition. The five archetypes \u2014 household transfers, job retention, business liquidity, health, investment \u2014 differ in demand, inflation, capacity-preservation, employment-shield and shock-attenuation weights. Spending arrives with inertia (last quarter\u2019s impulse still working).

3 \u00B7 Unemployment \u2014 Okun, shielded by furlough

U = U\u208B\u2081 \u2212 1.5\u00B7\u0394Y\u00B7(1 \u2212 shield) + 0.18\u00B7(U* \u2212 U\u208B\u2081)

Okun\u2019s law: output growth cuts unemployment. shield comes from the job-retention (furlough) share \u2014 it breaks the link between the output shock and layoffs, which is exactly the US\u2013EU divergence of 2020. U reverts gently toward its natural rate U* (4.0% US, 7.2% EU).

4 \u00B7 Inflation \u2014 expectations + the 3\u20136 quarter pipe

\u03C0 = 0.55\u00B7\u03C0\u208B\u2081 + 0.45\u00B72 + 0.09\u00B7Y\u208B\u2081 + laggedDemand + supplyShock + scarring \u2212 0.50\u00B7max(0, r\u22120.75)
laggedDemand = \u03A3 0.05\u00B7impulse\u209C for impulses 3\u20136 quarters old

Inflation is anchored partly on its past and partly on the 2% target, pushed by the output gap, and \u2014 crucially \u2014 by demand injected 3 to 6 quarters earlier. This lag is the core lesson: today\u2019s stimulus shows up in prices next year. Supply shocks (energy, reopening) and scarring add to it; rate hikes above 0.75% subtract, progressively. Why inflation still climbs late: the 2021 energy and reopening shock is exogenous — it happened whatever you do — and the demand you injected in 2020 is landing at exactly that moment. You cannot avoid the rise; you can decide how far it runs. A Fed that hikes early and firmly can hold end-2021 inflation near 4–5% instead of 7–8%, but it pays for that in a wider output gap and higher unemployment along the way — which the score counts, every quarter. That trade-off is the exercise.

5 \u00B7 Financial stress, equities, FX

stress = max(4, 0.45\u00B7stress\u208B\u2081 + 0.7\u00B7pandemicRisk \u2212 0.5\u00B7(QE_US+QE_EU))
S&P 500 = SPX\u208B\u2081 + 2.2\u00B7totalQE + 1.6\u00B7Y_US \u2212 0.7\u00B7\u0394stress \u2212 0.35\u00B7stress
STOXX 50 = STOXX\u208B\u2081 + 1.9\u00B7totalQE + 1.6\u00B7Y_EU \u2212 0.7\u00B7\u0394stress \u2212 0.35\u00B7stress \u2212 1.2\u00B7max(0, spread\u22121)

Why Europe usually lags \u2014 but need not. The two indices share the liquidity and stress channels, so they move together in a crisis. They diverge for three reasons: European equities track euro-area output, they carry a penalty when the periphery spread widens, and they respond slightly less to central-bank liquidity. A furlough-led Europe therefore tends to trail a transfers-led US, because furlough protects jobs rather than driving demand. It is not fixed, though: run stronger euro-area fiscal support with sustained ECB QE and a contained spread, and STOXX ends the game above the S&P. If Europe lagged in your game, that was a consequence of the policy mix, not a rule of the model.

FX(EUR/USD) = FX\u208B\u2081 + 0.006\u00B7QE_US \u2212 0.004\u00B7(Y_US \u2212 Y_EU)

QE calms stress and lifts equities; the pandemic shock raises stress. The relative Fed\u2013ECB stance moves the exchange rate.

6 \u00B7 Euro-area fragmentation \u2014 the spread loop

spread = max(0.4, 0.6\u00B7spread\u208B\u2081 + 0.05\u00B7(debt_EU \u2212 90) \u2212 0.22\u00B7QE_EU)

Rising periphery debt widens the spread; ECB QE compresses it. A high spread tightens financial conditions across the euro area regardless of the ECB\u2019s policy rate \u2014 a self-reinforcing loop the EU must actively fight.

Why the spread matters more than inflation for cohesion. Inflation is painful but shared: it hits Germany and Italy alike, and every member faces the same central bank response. The spread is different \u2014 it is a divergence in the price of money inside a single currency. When Italy borrows at 4% and Germany at 1%, one monetary policy produces two different economies: the same ECB rate is restrictive in the south and loose in the north. Sustained, that asks whether the currency union can hold at all \u2014 the question behind 2012\u2019s \u201Cwhatever it takes\u201D and behind the ECB\u2019s anti-fragmentation tools since. You can survive high inflation as a union; you cannot survive a permanent spread. That is why it is scored separately, and why the ECB seat is graded on it.

7 \u00B7 Animal spirits \u2014 the sentiment loop

raw = 0.6\u00B7sentiment\u208B\u2081 + 0.42\u00B7Y + 0.02\u00B7(equities\u2212100) \u2212 0.05\u00B7(stress\u221216) \u2212 0.35\u00B7max(0, spread\u22121)
sentiment = 10\u00B7tanh(raw / 10)

Confidence tracks realised output, markets and (inversely) stress, then feeds back into next quarter\u2019s demand (the 0.06\u00B7sentiment term in equation 1). Positive spirits amplify your stimulus; fear dilutes it. Set the gain to zero and the loop switches off.

8 \u00B7 Scoring

teamLoss = \u03A3\u2091 [ 1.0\u00B7avgInflGap + 0.7\u00B7avgUnempGap + 0.5\u00B7endOutputGap ] + 0.3\u00B7avgStress/10 + 0.6\u00B7max(0, peakSpread\u22121)
teamScore = clamp(100 \u2212 7\u00B7teamLoss, 0, 100)

Scored on stabilisation across the whole game \u2014 both economies near 2% inflation and full employment, penalised for stress and a blown spread. Unemployment counts every quarter, not just at the end. Each role is also scored against its own mandate. You are graded against stabilisation, never against copying what actually happened \u2014 reality wasn\u2019t optimal.

A full workbook of these formulas, with worked numbers, is available alongside the game.

How calibrated. Real Q1-2020 starting conditions for both blocs; under roughly the actual decisions the engine reproduces the real divergence (US unemployment spiking to double digits on layoffs while Europe’s furlough schemes hold it near 8%, and US inflation arriving earlier). It is a stylised reduced-form engine (IS / Phillips / Okun with a money, FX and fragmentation channel and explicit lags), not a forecast — the debrief lays your path against what actually happened so the differences are the lesson. Information is point-in-time: you see only what was knowable that quarter, with leading half-hints and lagged headline gauges.
